## 1. The ring of the shield breaks the promise of `safeRadius` — high — read and measured

**Where:** `DEFINITIONS.terrain.objects.layers` (`safeRadius`) in `src/data.js`; `createAvatar` and `finalShieldRadius` in `src/sim.js`; the relation in `AGENTS.md` ("Relations": the `safeRadius` of every layer should stay above the distance of a dash plus the avatar radius plus the largest object radius).

**What happens.** The avatar is born with a full shield, so its radius at the first frame is the ring (`bodyRadius x shield.ringScale`), not the body. The comment beside `safeRadius` still adds the body radius. With the ring, the sum is above `safeRadius`.

**Measured.** A straight dash from rest in an empty world, with the starter blueprint, covers about 147 px before it is back at top speed. For seeds 1 to 2000, an object that a first dash straight at it would touch exists in **32 seeds** with the ring, and in **0 seeds** with the body radius (the smallest gap with the ring is about 141 px). The script: for each seed, every object in a square of 260 px around the start, gap = distance of its center - its radius - the avatar radius, compared with the dash distance.

This is point 3.1 of the last review (the relation that only a comment tied), and the first time it bites: no test computes the dash distance.

**Recommendation (Rules desk).**
1. A test on the definitions, in the style of 101: dash distance of the starter blueprint + the avatar radius **with the shield full** + the largest radius of a layer < that layer's `safeRadius`. Break it once (it fails today).
2. Then a number: raise `safeRadius` with a reason that names the ring, or say in the data that the promise is now only for the body. `safeRadius` is a guess of an agent, not the user's number, so raising it needs no decision of his; telling him in one line is enough.
3. Write "with the shield full" into the relation in `AGENTS.md`.

## 2. The ring moves the reach of the sword and the birth of every hitbox — high — read

**Where:** `swingMelees` (the trigger: `distance - player.radius - target.radius > attack.reach`), `spawnHitbox` (the tip and the birth point: `owner.position + toward x owner.radius`) and `think` (the Biter's reach: `distance - actor.radius - player.radius`) in `src/sim.js`; the comment of `melee.reach` in `src/data.js` ("measured from the edge of the body").

**What happens.** `actor.radius` is the ring: it goes from the body (shield empty) to `bodyRadius x ringScale` (shield full). Every place above reads it, so:

- the sword starts its windup, and its cone is born, from the ring, not from the body. With a full shield its real reach from the body is longer by `bodyRadius x (ringScale - 1)` than with an empty one, and it shrinks as the shield is drained, during a fight;
- the bullets of the repeater are born at the ring;
- a Biter starts its bite when it is within `reach` of the ring.

The data says the reach of the sword is measured from the edge of the body, so the comment and the code disagree. The tests of the sword (161 to 167) play without a shield (`loadGame` removes it unless a test asks), so no test has ever seen the sword and the ring together.

For a Biter, reading the ring is in the spirit of the user's rule ("what is seen is what is hit"). For the avatar's own weapons it is a different question that nobody asked: does a sword reach from the mecha or from its shield?

**The user's answer (2026-11-13):** "they reach beyond the shield, yes; a hitbox the avatar creates hits neither the avatar nor the shield." The second half is already true in the code (`hitboxHurts`: the avatar's hitboxes hurt only what is not the avatar, and the shield is not a separate thing that could be hit). The agent's reading of the first half, **to confirm with him in one line**: the reach is counted from the edge of the shield, as the code does now, so with a full shield the sword reaches `bodyRadius x (ringScale - 1)` farther from the body than with an empty one.

**Recommendation (Rules desk).** Once the reading is confirmed: write the rule in `AGENTS.md` ("Weapons and equipment") in the user's words; fix the comment of `melee.reach` in `src/data.js` (it says "from the edge of the body"); add a test of the sword with the shield on (the reach is measured from the ring, and the cone never hurts the avatar). If he meant the other reading (from the body, always past the ring), the origin of a hitbox needs its own value (`bodyRadius` when the actor has one), in `spawnHitbox` and in the trigger, in one place.

## 3. The radius is both static and dynamic, and a second `finalizeStatic` inflates the body — medium — read and measured

**Where:** `finalizeStatic`, `finalRadius`, `writeShield` and `finalizeDynamic` in `src/sim.js`.

**What happens.** `finalRadius` returns `object.radius ?? definition.radius`, so it returns whatever is in the actor now. `writeShield` then copies `actor.radius` into `bodyRadius`. For the avatar, `actor.radius` is the ring after the first `finalizeDynamic`. So calling `finalizeStatic` again on a living avatar takes the ring as the new body, and the next `finalizeDynamic` makes a ring of the ring.

**Measured.** A new avatar of the combat blueprint, then `finalizeStatic` and `finalizeDynamic` called again, twice: the body goes 16 → 28.8 → 51.84 and the radius 28.8 → 51.84 → 93.312.

Nothing calls `finalizeStatic` twice today, so no run is wrong. But the user's rule says a value moves to the dynamic list "the day something makes it change during a run", and the first thing that does it (a part swapped, a transformation, a new life) will call the static list again. The radius is the one value that is in both lists, and `bodyRadius` is written as a side effect of the shield.

**Recommendation (Rules desk).** Make the body radius the static value and the radius only the dynamic one: `finalRadius` writes `bodyRadius` (from the roll or the recipe, never from `actor.radius`), and `finalizeDynamic` writes `radius` (the body, or the ring when there is a shield). Then a test: `finalizeStatic` called twice gives the same actor as once. It is a small change; the whole-state tests will say if a result moves.

## 4. The audit in the history costs more than the state — medium — measured

**Where:** `recordFrame` and `createHistory` in `src/history.js` (a copy of the audit per snap); `constants.audit.keepHits`.

**Measured.** Chamber 2, the combat blueprint, real data, 1800 frames walking in a square: a snap of the state is about 1.5 KB, the audit kept beside it about **24 KB** (the full list of the last `keepHits` hits). Recording a frame (the three copies) took about as long as the step itself (0.24 ms against 0.28 ms per frame). The 120 audits kept are about 2.9 MB; the 120 states about 0.2 MB.

The audit was made a separate object so the states stay cheap; the history then copies all of it every frame, so the cost came back through the side door. It grows with `keepHits`, not with what happened in the frame: the same hundred hits are copied 120 times.

**Recommendation (Rules desk; opinion on the shape).** The hits are written once and never change, so the history does not need copies of them: give each hit a serial number, and keep per snap only the serial of the last hit and a copy of the totals (small). Stepping back shows the hits whose serial is not above the one of that snap. Keep test 160 (the audit holds no truth) as the guard. Not urgent while the chambers are small, but it is the same trap as review 4.6, and the audit is what the bench and the model will read.

## 8. "The closest target" is measured to the center, the reach to the edge — low — read

**Where:** `closestTarget` (orders by distance between centers, filters by `range + actor.radius`) and the trigger of `swingMelees` (checks the gap between the bodies, for that one target only).

With Biters of different sizes, a larger one whose body is inside the reach can be ignored because a smaller one is closer by its center but outside the reach; the sword then waits. The window is the difference of the radii (a few pixels today), so it is rare now and grows with huge enemies.

**Recommendation.** A rule for the user when it comes up, not before: "closest" by the gap between the bodies or by the centers. The day it is decided, it is one line in `closestTarget`, and the bullets and the sword follow.

## 9. The shield's resistance also protects the part that goes through — low — read; a formula of the user

**Where:** `resolveDamage` (the shield's points are added when the shield is alive at the start of the hit) and `applyDamage` (the shield absorbs, the rest goes to health).

A hit that empties the shield is resisted, all of it, with the shield's points, including the part that then reaches health. In words: the hit is calculated once with the total, then split. This follows the user's words ("simple sum, and when it dies it just leaves the total"), so it is probably what he wants; it is just not written as a consequence anywhere.

**Recommendation.** When the shield's numbers are tuned, show him one hit as arithmetic (a hit larger than the shield that is left, with and without the shield's points on the part that goes through) and record his answer in "Damage". No code change unless he says so.

## 10. An avatar at zero health: hitboxes skip it, body slams do not — low — read

`resolveHitboxes` skips a target with no health; `collideWithObjects` and `touchActors` do not ask. So at zero health the avatar stops taking bites but still takes body slams, and its shield (if it regenerates) can only be hit by contact. The death of the avatar is not decided, so this is not a bug yet; it should be one rule when death is decided, not two accidents.
