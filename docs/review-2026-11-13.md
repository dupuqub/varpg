## 4. The audit in the history costs more than the state — medium — measured

**Where:** `recordFrame` and `createHistory` in `src/history.js` (a copy of the audit per snap); `constants.audit.keepHits`.

**Measured.** Chamber 2, the combat blueprint, real data, 1800 frames walking in a square: a snap of the state is about 1.5 KB, the audit kept beside it about **24 KB** (the full list of the last `keepHits` hits). Recording a frame (the three copies) took about as long as the step itself (0.24 ms against 0.28 ms per frame). The 120 audits kept are about 2.9 MB; the 120 states about 0.2 MB.

The audit was made a separate object so the states stay cheap; the history then copies all of it every frame, so the cost came back through the side door. It grows with `keepHits`, not with what happened in the frame: the same hundred hits are copied 120 times.

**Recommendation (Rules desk; opinion on the shape).** The hits are written once and never change, so the history does not need copies of them: give each hit a serial number, and keep per snap only the serial of the last hit and a copy of the totals (small). Stepping back shows the hits whose serial is not above the one of that snap. Keep test 160 (the audit holds no truth) as the guard. Not urgent while the chambers are small, but it is the same trap as review 4.6, and the audit is what the bench and the model will read.

## 8. "The closest target" is measured to the center, the reach to the edge — low — read

**Where:** `closestTarget` (orders by distance between centers, filters by `range + actor.radius`) and the trigger of `swingMelees` (checks the gap between the bodies, for that one target only).

With Biters of different sizes, a larger one whose body is inside the reach can be ignored because a smaller one is closer by its center but outside the reach; the sword then waits. The window is the difference of the radii (a few pixels today), so it is rare now and grows with huge enemies.

**Recommendation.** A rule for the user when it comes up, not before: "closest" by the gap between the bodies or by the centers. The day it is decided, it is one line in `closestTarget`, and the bullets and the sword follow.

## 9. The shield's resistance also protects the part that goes through — low — read; a formula of the user

**Where:** `resolveDamage` (the shield's points are added when the shield is alive at the start of the hit) and `applyDamage` (the shield absorbs, the rest goes to health).

A hit that empties the shield is resisted, all of it, with the shield's points, including the part that then reaches health. In words: the hit is calculated once with the total, then split. This follows the user's words ("simple sum, and when it dies it just leaves the total"), so it is probably what he wants; it is just not written as a consequence anywhere.

**Recommendation.** When the shield's numbers are tuned, show him one hit as arithmetic (a hit larger than the shield that is left, with and without the shield's points on the part that goes through) and record his answer in "Damage". No code change unless he says so.

## 10. An avatar at zero health: hitboxes skip it, body slams do not — low — read

`resolveHitboxes` skips a target with no health; `collideWithObjects` and `touchActors` do not ask. So at zero health the avatar stops taking bites but still takes body slams, and its shield (if it regenerates) can only be hit by contact. The death of the avatar is not decided, so this is not a bug yet; it should be one rule when death is decided, not two accidents.
