# Actor and the Dummy

## The Dummy

The user's idea (2026-11-10): a test actor that does not attack, made to see many things colliding. Numbers are in `src/data.js` (`DEFINITIONS.actors.dummy`, and the count in `DEFINITIONS.maps.chamber1.spawns`), with their reasons; nothing is repeated here.

- **Made by the seed, lives in the state, born from the spawn array (the user, 2026-11-15, issue 62: "os dummies tbm deveriam nascer no frame 0, não quero códigos não reutilizados ou reutilizáveis no jogo").** Chamber 1 has one spawn event, `{ initialFrame: 0, count, type: "dummy" }` in `DEFINITIONS.maps.chamber1.spawns`, and `spawnEvents` (stage 7b) makes them like any actor: `drawBirth` (radius, health, place), `finalizeObject("dummy", …)`, `spawnedActorId`. They follow every rule of issue 61: born in the step of frame 0, they exist from frame 1 (the state at frame 0 has only the avatar), ids are `dummy 0,<nth>` (event 0, nth 0 to count - 1), at the end of `state.actors`, a `birth` line each in the journal, their cells written at birth. **What a type rolls for itself (the one place):** `OWN_ROLLS` in `src/sim.js`, a table of functions by type; `spawnActor` calls the entry of the actor's type, if there is one, with the actor, the seed and the pair (event, nth), and the function writes into the actor what is its own. The Dummy's entry rolls its first angle (`SALTS.dummyAngle`, from the seed and the pair) and points it. The Biter has no entry. A future type adds one entry and no other code. **The agent's choices (the issue left the forms open; the smallest taken):** the table is code (a function cannot live in the frozen data); `wander` is `{ event, nth, angle, turns }` (the pair, not an index, which would repeat across two events), and a new angle is the hash of the hash of (seed, event, nth) with `turns`, so two Dummies of different events never share a sequence (test 247); `createChamberState` has no `populate` argument (every chamber starts with the avatar alone).
- **Where:** born like any actor (see "Birth" under "Actor"): anywhere off the screen and inside the window of the collision grid around the avatar, with no search for a free place (`birthOffset`, salts 17 and 18).
- **How it moves:** like any actor that moves (the user's decision, 2026-11-11: it has an `acceleration` and a `deceleration` in its recipe, and its velocity is calculated like anyone's): `wander.angle`, in whole degrees, gives the direction it wants, and its velocity goes there by its acceleration, in the same code as the avatar's. It is born still. A turn is therefore a curve, not a jump. Its deceleration is not used yet (a Dummy always wants to walk). `wander` is `{ event, nth, angle, turns }`. At every **new** contact (with an object that is still whole, with the avatar, or with another Dummy) it gets a new angle (the user's `rand(360)`, but from the seed, so the run is reproducible): the hash of the hash of (seed, event, nth) with `turns`, % 360 (`turnDummy`, `SALTS.dummyTurn`; tests 83 and 84). A pair that was already touching in the last frame still pushes it out, but does not turn it (the same contact rule as the avatar's hits).
- **Sine and cosine** come from our own series (`sinDegrees`, `cosDegrees`), with additions and multiplications only, because `Math.sin` may differ in the last digits between browsers.
- **Wrap:** a Dummy that leaves the window comes back on the opposite side (`wrapIntoWindow`), around the avatar wherever it is.
- **Only the avatar hurts a Dummy and only the avatar is hurt by it (the user's rule, 2026-11-10, see "Who hurts whom" in "Damage" (docs/rules/damage.md)), with the same body slam as the avatar's** (`finalAttack`, landed by `landHit`): at a new contact with the avatar whose impact reaches the minimum, the Dummy and the avatar each hurt the other with their own weight. The impact is the speed at which the two close in on each other (the difference of their velocities along the line between the centers). Against an object the Dummy is pushed out, slides or bounces like the avatar (see "Collision with still things") and turns; against another Dummy the two are pushed apart and turn; nobody is hurt in either, so a Dummy never breaks an object with weight (a weightless one breaks at its first contact with any actor: see "Weight 0" in "Damage" (docs/rules/damage.md); test 233). The Dummy weighs by the same rule as the objects (weight per radius times radius), from its own recipe. Health stops at 0, and an actor at 0 is removed at the end of the step (`removeDestroyed`; the avatar is the exception). A contact below the minimum impact turns the Dummy but hurts no one. The push between a Dummy and the avatar, or two Dummies, is split by weight (see "Push between actors by weight" in "The world: seed and objects").
- **Drawn** as blue circles by `drawActors` in `src/render.js` (the same function draws every actor but the avatar, by the color of its type). The window is twice the screen, so about a quarter of the Dummies are on screen at a time.
- Tests: 80 to 101 and 106 to 110, and 245 to 247 for the births, in `tests/agent/README.md`. Most tests play in a game with no Dummies (`count` 0 in the spawn event of a patched copy of the data); the Dummy tests call `firstBirths` on the new state, because a wandering Dummy pushes and hurts the avatar and would change their results; the Dummy tests use the real data (with 100 Dummies, to stay fast).

## Actor

An actor is any entity that can act in combat. Today the avatar, the Dummy and the Biter are the only ones. A thing without `move` (a trap, for example) would be a static actor, but none exists, so nothing in the game handles it.

**Attacks (the user, 2026-11-11):** every thing has `attacks`, a table of `{ base, now }` packets by type. Everyone has `bodySlam`; a recipe may add more (the Biter's `bite`, with `reach`, `windupFrames`, `freezeFrames` and a `hitbox` that are copied into the actor). The **base is made once** (`finalizeStatic`; for an attack whose recipe says so, flat + per-weight x weight, so weight is a term only where the recipe asks); **`now` is calculated again every frame from the weight the actor has then** (`finalizeDynamic`, `finalAttackNow`; the same recipe as the base, so it equals the base while the weight does not change, and the avatar's attacks get lighter as it fires its ammunition), and that is the place where an aura that gives its neighbors more attack will enter. The avatar's `resistance` is a table by type, rewritten every frame from the blueprint.

**The Biter (the first enemy with AI):** `actor.ai = { attack, phase, frames, strike }`. `think` runs before movement: in `chase` it walks straight at the avatar; when the space between the two bodies is within the reach of its attack it goes to `windup` (still for the frames the recipe says; the avatar can leave), then creates a hitbox (`think` decides the strike, `ai.strike`, and the hitbox is born later in the step, after everyone has moved, by `emitStrikes`: test 158) and goes to `freeze` (still again for the frames the recipe says), and then chases again. A `windupFrames` of 0 strikes at once (the user, 2026-11-11: a pause before and one after, only to prove both can exist; the style is crowds, so seeing the attack is not a priority). More behaviors are planned (keeping a distance, standing still, a windup before a hit, a windup before a dash) and will be new phases.

**Hitboxes (the user, 2026-11-11):** `state.hitboxes`, separate from the actors. **Decided (the user, 2026-11-14): what can be a target is an actor, what only hurts is a hitbox.** A bullet, a puddle and an aura are hitboxes (an aura and a puddle need two pieces a hitbox does not have yet: hurting the same target again every N frames, and staying with its owner); a totem is an actor, because it can be a target. The body of an actor is itself a hitbox (never a second one that follows it): the object inside the actor only tells how that body hurts (damage, ignored tags) and carries no radius, the radius is the actor's. The aim: one "hurting module" with the same field names in an actor and in a hitbox, read by the collision together with position and radius. **Rocks and Shrooms are actors too (the user, 2026-11-14):** a skill may want to aim at rocks one day (to blow them up), so they can be a target. In code they already are almost that: made by the same constructor (`finalizeObject`), with tags (`object`) and health (no resistances: a hit takes its damage whole), and hurt by contact and by shots. What differs is only where they live: they come from the formula of the seed, and only a hurt one is in the state (`state.damagedObjects`), which is what keeps the state and the history small. So they stay out of `state.actors`; what is missing for them to be a target is `pickTarget` looking at the objects near the asker when the tag asked is one they carry. Not built: it waits for the first skill that aims at them. Each is `{ owner, attack, position, radius, cone, framesLeft, damage, velocity, struck, pierceLeft, ignores, hits }` (`ignores`: the tags it does not hurt, a copy of its attack's list at its birth (test 180); see "Tags of exclusion"; `hits`: how it hits, `"first"` or `"all"`, a copy of its attack's key; see "Shots and objects" (docs/rules/weapons.md)): a circle (or a cone, the sword's), born toward the target by **one function, `spawnHitbox`** (the bite of a Biter, the bullet of a weapon and the cone of a sword all use it), at the place **`hitboxOrigin`** says: **the exit of the attack of the avatar is `center`: its hitboxes are born at its center, and its reach is counted from there; neither the body nor the ring of the shield changes it** (the user, 2026-11-13: "the reach of the sword stays untouched; the point of origin is the center of the avatar/actor"; test 170). **The bite has the exit `front`** (the user, 2026-11-13, issue 3: "the Biter can stay the same, the attack comes out of the front"; it is a key of the attack, not a rule about the avatar): its bite is born one body ahead of it, toward its target, and its reach stays the gap between the two bodies (test 121); it keeps **the final packet of the attack as it was at birth**, hurts each target once (`struck`), moves by its `velocity` (zero for a bite) and lives its frames (`framesLeft`; null for a bullet, which lives until it leaves the grid, see "Weapons and equipment" (docs/rules/weapons.md)); `pierceLeft` is how many more things it may cross (null for a hitbox that does not end on a hit; see "Weapons and equipment" (docs/rules/weapons.md)). `hitboxHurts` is the side rule (the avatar's hitboxes hurt everyone else, anyone else's hurt the avatar). More behaviors (it follows its owner, arcs, lines) are preparation only, in `docs/weapon-families.md`; the size of the radius is fixed per attack for now (whether it should follow the size of the attacker is open).

**Destruction (the user, 2026-11-11):** `destroyActor` and `removeDestroyed` take an actor with no health out of the scene and out of the state, and it is no longer drawn. It does nothing else yet (no drop, no effect). The avatar is not removed: at zero health it ends the mission (the user, 2026-11-14): `state.mission` is `"running"` when the state is made and becomes `"aborted"` at the end of the step in which the avatar has no health (phase 8, after the frame is counted); `step` then returns at its first line, so the state, the visual state and the journal stay as they were, and `recordFrame` does not record a state whose frame is the last snap's. Whoever has no health is never a target (`pickTarget`). Tests 190 to 193. **The avatar's death stops the world (the user, 2026-11-14, issue 28: "if I died, nothing of mine takes effect after the test of my death, and the world stops"):** nothing of the avatar takes effect after the death is tested, and the rest of that step runs for nobody. Inside the one contact that kills, both sides still hurt each other, so the death is tested after a whole contact, never in the middle of it. One function, `worldStops(state)`, is asked after each contact with an object (stage 2), after each contact between actors (stage 4), after each hit of a hitbox (stage 5) and in phase 8. When it is true the rest of stages 2 to 5 is skipped (`playStages` returns early; `resolveHitboxes` stops after the hitbox that killed, which still loses its frame) and the step goes to its end: `removeDestroyed`, the shield and the frame counter still run, so a death by a bite gives the state it always gave, except for the hitboxes after the one that killed. What is skipped and kept, with the reasons, is in `docs/step-order.md`. Tests 209 to 211. **After the death (the user, issue 42, comments of 2026-11-14 and 2026-11-15):** the screen's buttons stay and the state freezes (only the frames stop). Back and forward still walk the saved snaps, and the last snap of the history is the aborted one (`recordFrame` does not record a state that did not advance). Pressing play on a snap from before the death goes on from there: `stateFromSnap` gives it as `running` again, so the death is undone, and `step` plays on (the same inputs on the same frame give the same death; other inputs give another state). It is a debugger for now: in the finished game the death will not be undoable by going back in time (later). The combat log shows what happened, and only grows: the `aborted` line (issue 58) and then, after going back and playing, the `resume` line; `discardAfter` cuts the history, never the log. Tests 222 to 225 keep it.

An actor **in the state** holds **its own value for everything it uses** (the user's rule, 2026-11-10: "always check the object itself", everything can change in a run: a druid that becomes a giant bear, a bigger mecha): `id`, `type`, `radius`, `position`, `cells` (see "Collision grid"), `health` (`{ now, max }`, the user's mold: almost everything has a now and a max), and, if it has them, `move` (`direction`, `velocity`), `acceleration`, `deceleration` and `dash` (`charges: { now, max }`, `recharge`, `rechargeFrames`). **Every final value is calculated by a final function and written into the actor, and the rest of the code reads the actor (the user's rules, 2026-11-11). When it is calculated depends on what it is: what is static, once, when the actor is made at the start of the run (`finalizeStatic`: radius (the body; an actor with a shield keeps it apart in `bodyRadius`, because its `radius` is dynamic and follows the shield, so calling the static list again never takes the ring for the body: test 171), weight (the avatar's is calculated again every frame in `finalizeDynamic`, because it follows the ammunition it carries: test 79), bounce, acceleration, deceleration, the maximum health, the recharge time and the maximum of charges of the dash); what is dynamic, again every frame, at the start of `step` (`finalizeDynamic`: the top speed, the resistance, the attacks' `now`, the dash speed).** Which value is in which list is written in those two functions; a value moves to the dynamic list the day something makes it change during a run. The user confirmed the dynamic list (2026-11-11): the top speed must be calculated every frame, and the resistance and the dash speed are potentially dynamic, because items and other things can affect them. The definitions only hold the base (and the parameters of creation); the shape of each type is its `initial` in `src/data.js`. The maximum health, resistance, top speed and dash speed of the avatar come from the parts of its blueprint. An object (rock, Shroom) also holds its own `weight` and `bounce` (the user's decision, 2026-11-10: "use the formula when making the object, and then it becomes the reference"): `finalizeObject` computes them from its own radius with `finalWeight` and `finalBounce`, and the collision reads `object.weight` and `object.bounce`, never the definition. The avatar holds its `weight` too (the weight of the parts of its blueprint and of the rounds in its own weapon state: `finalWeight`, test 143) and a `bounce` of 0. The attack (`finalAttack`) reads the `weight` of the attacker.

**Draws and ids of an actor born from a spawn event (issue 60, rules 2/4; built; `spawnActor` calls it since issue 61).** The user decided: an actor born from the map's array is identified by (seed, index of the event in the array, nth actor of that event), with no retry counter: one draw, born there, and the collision pushes it out if it lands on something. `drawBirth(seed, type, event, nth, grid)` returns the radius, health and birth offset, all from `hashInts(seed, event, nth, salt)`, and `birthOffset(seed, event, nth, radius, grid)` takes the pair. **The agent's choice (the issue left the shape open):** the id is `spawnedActorId(type, event, nth)` = `<type> <event>,<nth>`, for example `biter 3,2`, made from the pair alone and never from a counter or the length of the list, so it is unique for the pair and a removed actor's id never comes back. The space is the smallest form that keeps `combatJournalTotals` right: it strips ONE trailing `_<number>` and then everything from the first space, so `biter 3,2` is the kind `biter` and the totals still say `biter:bite` (`biter_3_2` would have been `biter_3`); it is the same shape as the places of objects (`rock 12,4`). Tests 235 to 237.

**Birth (the user's rule, 2026-11-10, `birthOffset` in `src/sim.js`):** an actor is born anywhere **off the screen and inside the window** of the grid, with **no search for a free place**. The birth ground is the ring between the screen and the edge of the window: the whole body off the screen (the center at least its radius beyond the edge of the screen), the center inside the window (the same rule as the wrap: the body may stick out, the center may not). Every point of the ring is equally likely (the ring is four strips, one is picked with a chance equal to its area, then a point in it), and the numbers come from the seed and the pair (event, nth) of the actor (test 235), so a run is reproducible. If it lands on something, the push of the collision separates them over the next steps, like an actor that wraps onto another; an object in the way is not removed (that is what `crushes` is for, when the actor has it). The ring always has room, because test 101 keeps every radius below the margin between the screen and the edge of the window. The order of birth the user gave: the things of the seed exist first (they are a formula, so they always do), the moving actors after. Every actor is born this way, at the end of the step of its frame (`spawnEvents`, stage 7b, issues 61 and 62: the Dummies at frame 0 too), from the avatar's place at that moment.

**Crushing (the user's decision, 2026-11-11, `crushObject` in `src/sim.js`):** a **potential behavior of every actor**, on or off: every actor in the state carries `crushes`, a plain boolean (the same box for all, the user's rule: every actor can acquire a characteristic that other actors can). Today it is off for everyone (the avatar and the Dummy have it in their `initial`), and nothing switches it on; the user's picture is a **buff with a time**, like an aura, that turns it on and off, and that time belongs to the buff, not to this field. An actor that crushes **breaks every object it touches**: the object vanishes (`breakObject`, marked at zero in `damagedObjects`, exactly like an object that reached zero health; no new state, the user chose this over flinging it), no damage goes either way, the actor is not pushed, not bounced, not turned (a Dummy keeps its angle), and keeps going; it needs no minimum impact. It is a rule and not a huge damage in disguise, so the damage trace must show it as a rule. It concerns objects only: a crusher meets other actors like anyone. The first users will be the giants, to help them walk, but it is not theirs. Cost to know: every crushed object adds an entry to `damagedObjects`, so a crusher that crosses much ground grows the state (bounded by the ten minutes of a map). Tests 106 to 108.
