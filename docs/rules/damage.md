# Damage
## Damage

```
packet     = { blunt: n, puncture: n, slash: n }        (only the types above zero; the order of DEFINITIONS.damageTypes)
reduction  = points / (points + resistanceScale)  (per type, from the target's resistance)
after      = amount x (1 - reduction)             (per type)
DAMAGE     = sum of after (an empty packet is 0; there is no minimum damage)
           (points below 0 give a negative reduction and the damage grows; points + resistanceScale must stay above 0)
HEALTH     = HEALTH - DAMAGE
```

One function lands every hit (`landHit`: resolve, take off the actor or the object, record in the journal); the body slams and the hitboxes only call it.

- **Damage is a packet by type (the user's design, 2026-11-11).** A hit carries one amount per type and the types stay independent all the way: a sword of fire against a creature resistant to fire is still a blade (the blunt or puncture part is resisted on its own). Three types exist now (blunt, puncture and slash, the sword's); the list is `DEFINITIONS.damageTypes` and its order is the order of calculation.
- **Resistance is in points per type and the curve is hyperbolic (the user's decision: "a curve, no immortality, no hyper resistance", a ratio the agent proposed and the user accepted for now):** the share removed is `points / (points + resistanceScale)`. It never reaches 100%, and every extra point still helps, with diminishing returns. The numbers are to be seen in play.
- **A negative resistance amplifies the damage (the user, 2026-11-15: "negative resistance amplifies damage"; issue 34, tests 228 to 231).** The curve is unchanged, so negative points are a negative reduction: with the scale 100, -50 points is x2 and -75 is x4. **That the amplification grows without limit as the points go toward minus the scale is the agent's reading of the formula, not the user's choice;** he may want a cap. Points plus the scale at or below 0 cannot exist: refused when the data loads and in `assertValidBlueprint`, and `resolveDamage` throws an Error naming the type and the points if it ever sees them (a future buff cannot reach `NaN` or a negative damage unseen). `applyDamage` and the overflow of `landHit` need no change: the shield takes at most what it has, health stops at 0, and the share that did not fit is resolved again against the body alone. The agent's reading too: the blueprint is checked with the body's sum alone as well as with the shield's added, since a broken shield leaves the body's sum.
- **There is no minimum damage (the user, 2026-11-14):** he mentioned a minimum once only to illustrate the curve he wanted (armor must never make an actor invincible); the curve already guarantees it, since it never takes everything off, so there is no key of a minimum in the data. Health stops at zero; **an actor at zero health is removed from the scene at the end of the step by `removeDestroyed`, except the avatar, which stays in the scene and ends the mission instead** (`state.mission` becomes `"aborted"`, see "Destruction" under "Actor").
- **`resolveDamage(packet, target)` returns the record of a hit** (`parts` with type, amount, points, reduction and the amount after; `total`). **It is written down in the combat journal (the user, 2026-11-13; built, see the next point).** Every damage goes through `slamActor`, `slamObject` or the hit of `resolveHitboxes`, so a new source of damage has to record in the same way (`recordHit`). `applyDamage` takes the health and says what it took from the shield and from health.
- **The combat journal (the user, 2026-11-13; BUILT).** An external observer's notebook, **outside the state, the visual state and the history**: a plain array of text lines (`createJournal()` makes `[]`, `src/main.js` keeps the live one, in the console `journal`). The rules **only push to it and never read it** (test 160: the same state with it and without it), it **only grows and has no limit** (inside one chamber: **each chamber starts a new log**, the user, 2026-11-13, issue 18), and the history keeps no copy of it (test 174). **One line per damage calculation** (one attack on one target: a sword that hits three Biters writes three lines; an attack that touches nobody writes none, and a hit of 0 on a target is a hit and writes its line: see the next point), written by `recordHit` with `combatJournalHitLine`: JSON on one line, `{ frame, event: "hit", source, attack, target, parts, total, shield, health }` (plus `overflow` on a hit that broke the shield); `source` and `target` are actor ids (an object is its type and place); `shield` and `health` are what the target really lost, so an overkill counts only the health it had, for an actor and for an object alike (issue 29, test 204); `frame` is the frame the screen shows when the hit is seen (the state after its step: review 2026-11-13, point 6, test 159). Two descriptive lines come from the screen (`src/main.js`, test 175): `{ frame, event: "start", run, chamber }` when a chamber starts, and `{ frame, event: "resume" }` on **every play after a pause**, with the frame the simulation resumes from, so the same actor dying twice can be understood. The rules write two more kinds (issue 61): `{ frame, event: "birth", spawn, id, type, position }` for every actor born from a spawn event, and `{ frame, event: "error", error: "unknown type", map, spawn, type }` for a spawn event skipped because its type does not exist (`combatJournalBirthLine`, `combatJournalSpawnErrorLine`; the frame is the one the newborn is first seen in, `state.frame + 1`, as for a hit). The third descriptive line is `{ frame, event: "aborted" }`, written by the screen once, right after the step in which the mission became aborted (the user, 2026-11-15, issue 58; `combatJournalAbortedLine`, test 221). **Lines are written only when a new state is calculated**: stepping back or forward through saved snaps writes nothing; playing on from a past frame writes again (the resume line says so), so a frame number can repeat and the same hit can appear any number of times. The **totals are read from the lines** (`combatJournalTotals(lines)`, test 173: by `kind:attack`, e.g. `biter:bite`, `avatar:repeater-arm`, `avatar:bodySlam`, `rock:bodySlam`: hits, damage, shield, health); the button COMBAT REPORT of Chamber 0 plays the combat blueprint for 900 frames and prints the totals and the last 10 lines. Its tests: 172 the lines, 173 the totals, 159 and 160 the log written by the rules, 174 the history, 175 the screen lines. **Not done, on purpose, until buffs and skills exist:** the origin of each number (which part, skill or buff gave it) in the line. **Its names in the code (the user, 2026-11-15, issue 59):** `createJournal`, the `journal` argument of `recordHit` and the 5th of `step`, `combatJournal*Line`, `combatJournalTotals`, `journal` in `src/main.js` and `src/chamber-0.js`.
- **A hit that carries no damage is still a hit (the user, 2026-11-14, issue 46, his option b):** a contact happened, it was only not effective, and it may count for other metrics later. A hit of 0 (an empty packet: a body of weight 0, or an attack whose recipe gives 0) writes its journal line with a total of 0, and the totals count it as a hit; a bullet marks the target as struck, spends a pierce and, with pierce 0, ends on it (a Rock that loses nothing still stops it). It is not damage, so it does not restart the shield's wait (`applyDamage` asks for damage above 0). Test 220. Each Branch the avatar breaks writes two lines: the Branch's slam on the avatar (total 0) and the avatar's hit on the Branch.
- **A target with no health takes no hit, whoever hits** (one rule, `canTakeHit`, asked at the top of `landHit` and nowhere else since issue 32: an actor at 0, an object broken; test 194 for the body slam, 205 for `landHit`). A hitbox whose hit does not land marks no `struck` and spends no pierce. `objectsNear` still leaves out broken objects, because a broken object is gone for the collision too.
- **One list, one copy, one check for the keys every attack shares (issue 33, test 206):** `ATTACK_KEYS` (target, exit, ignores, hits; the body slam aims at nothing and has none of them, so it has no list of its own since issue 45; a hitbox keeps `HITBOX_ATTACK_KEYS`, the ignored tags and how it hits), `attackKeys` copies them into a record (`addWeaponAttacks`, `spawnHitbox`; `finalAttacks` copies its whole recipe in its own order), and `checkAttackKeys` checks them in the three places an aimed recipe lives: the bite, the weapon and the melee (the list of ignored tags is checked with the other lists of tags). A new key of the action model goes into the list and into `CHECK_ATTACK_KEY`, once.
- **An attack record has no `repeat` and no `scale` key (the user, 2026-11-14, issue 45; data version 60).** The "once" of a body is `state.contacts`, that of a hitbox is `struck`, and the growth with the impact is written inside `finalAttack`. How often a hitbox hurts is its tag `hits` (issue 43, see "Shots and objects") and the basic rule below; the weight term is `perWeight`, which each attack writes. **The impact keeps multiplying the body slam only, inside its own formula.** Do not bring the keys back until a case needs a second value (an aura or a puddle that hurts again every N frames).
- **Basic rule (the user, issue 45): an actor is never hurt twice by the same hitbox in the same frame.** It is what `struck` already does (the first hit marks the target; no switch, no code of its own).
- **Only a melee attack may use the weight (the user, issue 45).** `assertValidDefinitions` refuses a `perWeight` above 0 on an attack that is not melee (`checkNoWeight`). Melee here: the body slam of the constants, the attacks of an actor's recipe (the bite) and the `melee` of a part. Not melee: the `weapon` of a part; its projectile writes its damage as a plain number per type, so it cannot carry a weight term, and the `{ flat, perWeight }` form with a `perWeight` above 0 is refused there (test 212).
- **Who hurts whom (the user's rule, 2026-11-10; since 2026-11-13 it is data, the tags of exclusion, `bodyHurts` in `src/sim.js`, each direction on its own, test 179): with today's recipes the body slam hurts only where the avatar is one of the two sides, in both directions.** (An actor that crushes is the exception for objects: see "Crushing" under "Actor". **So is a thing of weight 0, which any actor's body breaks, the Biter's too (the user, 2026-11-15, issue 47, I27): see "Weight 0".** The rule that an actor never breaks an object stays for things with weight.) The avatar hurts actors and objects, and actors and objects hurt the avatar. An actor against an object, an object against an actor, or two actors: a collision (push, bounce, turn) but no hit, nobody is hurt, and so an actor never breaks an object. The reason is the user's: the weight of the mecha is the center of the damage of contact, which makes the weight interesting; what else fights will be the weapons (and `crushes`, see "Actor"). Test 90 plays a long run and checks that nothing is hurt without the avatar.
- **Body slam (the user's decisions, with his first numbers, "to begin with", not validated by play):** a collision that hurts (see above) hurts **both sides**, each with **its own weight**. Every **collision** is a hit (the user's decision: modular, because speeds will change). There is **no `doesDamage` flag** (the user removed it as a relic of an idea that died): what is harmless is decided by the weight alone, a weight of 0 has an attack of 0 (see "Weight 0"). **Only the moment of the collision hurts, friction does not** (the user's decision): a pair that was already touching in the last frame is friction, and `state.contacts` keeps the pairs of the last frame to tell them apart. Pressing and sliding still push and bounce, but are not new hits. The attack grows with the weight and the impact: the **body slam is an attack like any other: a blunt packet** (the user, 2026-11-11: it is the foundation of why attacks are packets), flat + perWeight x the weight of the attacker as the base, written in the data as `bodySlam.attack`, a recipe like any other attack (no special case in the code), scaled at the hit by `impact / bodySlam.referenceImpact` (**the formula is the agent's provisional proposal, to be discussed with the user**; the two constants are his first numbers). The impact is the part of the speed going into the other thing, so a graze is a weak hit. The weight of the avatar is what it carries now: the loaded weight of its blueprint (parts and all the ammunition) minus the rounds it has fired, calculated again every frame (`carriedWeight`); of anything else, `weightPerRadius` x radius of its definition. Later modifiers of the body slam do not exist yet.
- **Weights are whole numbers (the user's rule):** the `weight` of a part, and for an object the `weightPerRadius` and the radii of its `size`, are whole numbers, zero or more, so the weight is whole by construction. `assertValidDefinitions` in `src/sim.js` checks it when the file loads and fails loudly, so a float cannot get into the sums.
- **Weight 0 (the user's idea; the rule of 2026-11-15, issue 47: "things with no weight break immediately; health is not a parameter for what weighs 0"):** an object with weight 0 (a `weightPerRadius` of 0) is harmless and **breaks at the first contact** with the body of any actor (see "It breaks with anything" below), whatever its health and with no minimum impact (`breakWeightless` in `src/sim.js`, asked in `collideWithObjects` right after `crushObject`). It works like crushing: no push, no bounce, the mover keeps its speed and goes on; an avatar that starts in touch with it breaks it on the next step. No rule reads its health: the health stays in the data only because every object has the box `{ now, max }`. The contact writes its journal lines (hits of 0 are hits, see above): the object's slam on the actor (total 0) where `bodyHurts` says it hurts it (the avatar), then the actor's hit on it (a hit of total 0 for a body that does not hurt objects, I28); the object breaks whatever that hit took. **It breaks with anything (the user, 2026-11-15, after I24: "the Branch has to break with anything. It is only a visual mark for entertainment, to see things breaking. Today it is a Branch, but it is a placeholder for the things we want to break to show mechas and huge monsters as machines of destruction"):** the body of ANY actor (the avatar, a Dummy, a Biter) breaks it at the first contact, whether or not that body hurts objects: breaking it is not damage, health is not a parameter, so `breakWeightless` does not ask `bodyHurts` whether the body hurts it. **Everything that breaks it writes a line (the user, 2026-11-15, I28: "we have to know why projectiles were lost"):** a body that ignores `object` (the Biter's, the Dummy's) lands an empty packet on it through `landHit`, a hit of total 0 from that actor on the Branch (no invented damage), so the contact of every kind of actor leaves a line; the object breaks and the actor goes through at its speed. A bullet or hitbox that ends on it already leaves its hit line (source the owner, attack the weapon, target `branch <place>`, the total), so the loss of a projectile can be read (test 234). And any hit that lands on it (a bullet, a hitbox that does not ignore `object`, a body slam) breaks it whatever the damage resolved to, a hit of 0 too (`landHit`, test 232); the hit itself follows the usual rules (the coordinator's reading, not the user's words: a bullet spends its pierce or stops as on any hit, so a bullet of pierce 0 ends on the Branch). In reality nothing weighs 0, but for a mecha it is the same. **"Any actor breaks it" holds today because every actor is tangible (the user, 2026-11-16, issue 65): a future intangible actor (for example one hurt only by elemental damage) must not break a weightless thing.** No field and no code until that actor exists.
  **The Branch is the first weight 0 thing (the user, 2026-11-14, issue 51):** an object of weight 0 and health 1 that the seed places in 1 of 10 squares with an object (`rareKinds` of the layer, a roll of its own, `SALTS.objectRare`, so every other square keeps the kind and numbers it had; `kindInSquare`). Most tests play without Branches (`loadGame`, option `branches`), because the rock at 99,456 they use became one; tests 207, 208, 220, 226 and 227 use the real layer. Drawing it is the Screen desk's (parcel S3).
- **The list of objects (the user's idea):** boxes, rocks, logs, houses and so on are all the same kind of thing, listed in the library as `DEFINITIONS.objects`. **Each entry is complete and has its own numbers; nothing is shared between entries (the user's rule, critical):** there are many objects and every one must be unique, so no value may come from a constant that serves two entries. **Each entry is only ranges (the user's rule):** `size: { min, max }` (the radius range), `healthRange: { min, max }`, `weightPerRadius`, and what is its own (the Shroom has a `bounce` `{ min, max }`). The entries are the **creation parameters** that the seed rolls for each object placed; an object has no fixed number and no initial state. **Weight follows the radius (the user's decision, because it is easier to read and to balance, and the range of weights is as wide as the range of health):** `weight = weightPerRadius x radius`, both whole numbers, nothing rounded; the weight is computed once, by `finalWeight` inside `finalizeObject`, and stored on the object (`object.weight`). Every object has its own `radius` and carries its own `weight` and `bounce`.
- **The Shroom (the user's idea, a test item):** the object `shroom`: like the rock in the numbers the user gave (the same values, but written in its own entry, not shared), with a bounce that grows with the radius. The seed places it like the rock (`kinds` has both, with the same chance: a guess). Its `healthRange` is a guess, to be tuned by playing. The avatar's body slam hurts it and it hits back with its own weight; it bounces the avatar back by its own `bounce` (made by `finalBounce`).
- **Shield (the user's decisions, 2026-11-11 and 12; built):** **a health that regenerates**, and it comes primarily from the Generator (its specs `shieldMax`, `shieldDelayFrames`, `shieldRegenPerFrame` and `shieldResistance`, summed over the parts; the numbers and their reasons are in `src/data.js`). Order: the damage is calculated first (`resolveDamage`), **`applyDamage` makes the shield absorb it first, and what is left goes to health**. The avatar carries `shield: { now, max, quiet, delayFrames, regenPerFrame, resistance }`: `max`, the wait, the rate and the resistance are static (`writeShield`, at the start of the run); `now` and `quiet` (the frames since the last damage) are state. **Any damage, absorbed or not, sets `quiet` to 0**; at the end of every step (`regenerateShield`) `quiet` counts up to `delayFrames`, and once the wait is over the shield gives back `regenPerFrame` per frame up to `max`. **While the shield has something left it adds its `resistance` (points per type) to the total** (the user: "simple sum, and when it dies it just leaves the total"): `resolveDamage` adds it per hit, so the record says how many points came from the shield (`fromShield`). **A hit that breaks the shield (the user, 2026-11-14):** the shield's resistance counts only for the part the shield absorbs; the share of the packet that is left is resolved again against the body alone (`landHit`, test 202). That share is one number for the whole packet, the same for every type, so no type empties the shield first. **The ring (the user's idea):** the avatar's `radius`, the one every collision and hit reads, is dynamic and goes in a straight line from the body (`bodyRadius`, shield empty) to the body times `constants.shield.ringScale` (shield full), with the share of the shield that is left (`finalShieldRadius`, in `finalizeDynamic`); the ring is **drawn** at that radius, so what is seen is what is hit. The radius can jump when one hit takes much shield at once; that is its nature (the user). **No body slam comes from the ring growing, with no step of its own:** a hit needs an impact, and growing is not one (test 149).
- **Damage trace (the user's requirement, not implemented):** any change to health must be explainable. The damage is shown as a formula such as `(X + Y + Z) * W`, and every term says where it came from (which skill, part or buff). It feeds the damage audit page. The structure of the trace is not defined.
- The formula will be developed little by little, one piece at a time.
- **Known weaknesses (for the user to decide):**
  - The numbers of resistance and of the Biter's bite are guesses, to be tuned by playing; to be seen in play.
  - A bullet that enters and leaves a thing inside one frame is not seen: `resolveHitboxes` takes what a hitbox touches where its frame ends, so the outer part of the chord the path cuts is missed; for the smallest pair today (a repeater bullet of radius 6 at 8 px per frame against a Branch of radius 6) it is the outer 0.7 px of the 12 px that touch.
  - Hits come in whole steps of the attack, so a fine health range for the rocks shows up in play as only a few different numbers of hits.
