# Movement and input

## In short

- The game view fills the whole window, keeping its shape, and the camera follows the avatar, which stays at the center of the screen (docs/rules/movement.md).
- The player only moves (keys, the left stick or a finger) and dashes; there is no manual aim, because the weapons aim by themselves (docs/rules/movement.md).
- Movement has inertia: the avatar speeds up toward where it wants to go and slides to a stop, and its top speed is worked out again every frame from its parts and the weight it carries (docs/rules/movement.md).
- A dash spends a charge and throws the avatar in the direction of its movement at full strength, even with a half-tilted stick; above the top speed it slows by a constant amount, so a stronger dash is also a longer one (docs/rules/movement.md).
- The charges and their recharge come from the parts of whoever dashes, and the dash is one formula for every actor (the Jumper moves only by dashing); a dash with no direction does nothing and keeps the charge, a stand-in for a planned blast that destroys projectiles (docs/rules/movement.md).
- A dash turns on the key of the jump (`dash.active`), which turns off when the speed is back at or below the top speed; a push never turns it on, and it changes nothing of the movement (docs/rules/movement.md).
- On a touch screen, one half is a floating stick and the whole other half is the dash; the gamepad stick wins over touch, and touch over the keys (docs/rules/movement.md).
- The input of each frame is the movement and whether the dash was pressed in that frame, and it is saved in the history (docs/rules/movement.md).
- The game runs at most one step per frame of the browser: a faster monitor does not speed it up, and a slow machine plays it in slow motion (docs/rules/movement.md).

## Movement and input

The user approved this and noted that everything may change.

- The canvas is **1920 x 1080** (the user's requirement), and world units are canvas pixels. **Chamber 1 is a full-window game view** (the user's requirement: the whole screen is playable): the canvas fills the window and keeps its shape, with black bars if the window has another shape. A small faint label in the top-left corner shows the title, the controls and a BACK button; Esc also goes back.
- The avatar is always at the center of the screen (the camera follows it), drawn as a white circle.
- Move with WASD or the left analog stick. **There is no manual aim** (the user's decision, no mouse and no right stick): the weapons aim by themselves. So the avatar has no `aim` field; a weapon's target is data of its attack (`target`, `{ pick, tag }`, issue 13, test 183), read by `pickTarget`.
- `move.direction` is a vector with length from zero to one. On the keyboard, diagonals are normalized so they are not faster (not confirmed by the user; his old project used another factor).
- Vectors are used instead of angles, so the simulation needs no trigonometry, which can differ between browsers.
- **Movement has inertia** (the user's requirement): the avatar speeds up and slides to a stop. `move.direction` is what the actor *wants*, and `move.velocity` is how it is *really* moving; the velocity has memory, so it is state. Every frame `approach(velocity, target, rate)` moves the velocity in a straight line toward `direction x finalSpeed`, by at most `rate`, and lands exactly on the target when it is within reach. `rate` is the `acceleration` of the actor while it wants to move and its `deceleration` when it does not. Turning around passes through zero, with no jump. Only arithmetic and `Math.sqrt`, so it stays deterministic. The values are exact binary fractions on purpose, so the sums are exact.
- **Top speed is dynamic** (the user's requirement): `finalSpeed(actor, blueprint)` is asked again every frame and stored in the actor (`actor.speed`).
- **Dash** (Space or the gamepad A button, the user's choice), in `updateDash`. It spends one charge and sets the velocity to the direction (always at full length, so a half-tilted stick dashes just as hard) times `finalDashSpeed`. The frame of the dash already moves at the full dash speed. While an actor is faster than its top speed, its speed falls by the **constant** `overspeedDecay` every frame, down to the top speed, and then normal movement takes over. **Any part may add to the dash strength, but the decay is a constant** (the user's decisions), so a stronger dash is also a longer dash. The dash state is `actor.dash = { charges: { now, max }, recharge, active, rechargeFrames }`, carried by the actor; the dash numbers (strength, maximum of charges, recharge time) are read from the parts of whoever dashes: the avatar's blueprint (the Booster) or a creature's own parts (`dashFromParts`). `OVERSPEED_TOLERANCE` keeps the rounding error of a diagonal from counting as "faster than the top speed".
- **The dash is one formula for every actor** (the user, issue 76: "deveria ser a mesma para todos os atores"): `updateDash`, the dash specs of a part and the decay above the top speed are the same for the avatar and for a creature; only who presses changes. The avatar dashes when the button is pressed; an actor whose mind jumps (the Jumper) dashes whenever it wants to move. **The Spring legs give the Jumper its dash:** the Booster's dash strength, 1 charge and 90 frames of recharge, with speed, acceleration and deceleration 0, so it moves only by dashing and slides to a stop after each jump (the button-football look the user described). The charges and the recharge are guesses of the Review desk, to tune by playing; the strength is the Booster's by choice. Where the Jumper aims is in "Actor" (docs/rules/actor.md).
- **The key of the jump** (the user, issue 81: "o pulo vira uma chave bool quando inicia, essa chave só vira 0 quando a velocidade atual é igual ou menor que a máxima"): `actor.dash.active`, a boolean of every actor with a dash, the avatar too, born `false`. **Only the start of a dash sets it** (`updateDash`); it is set `false` where the movement of the actor ends in the step (stage 2, after the objects: `releaseDashKey`), when its speed is at or below its top speed (with `OVERSPEED_TOLERANCE`). A push alone never sets it (a push between actors moves only positions); a push during a jump leaves it on until the speed is back. "A chave não afeta a velocidade": nothing of the movement reads it, it only records. **There is no number of frames**: the jump lasts as long as the speed takes to fall back. It costs 15 bytes per snap per actor with a dash in the JSON of a snap (`,"active":false`; 14 when `true`).
- A dash without a direction does nothing and keeps the charge. This is a stand-in: **the user's plan (not developed) is that a dash without a direction becomes an EMP that destroys projectiles**, on the same button and the **same charge** (the lore: the overflow of the dash charge leaking out of the mecha's exhaust).
- The input is `{ move, dash }`, where `dash` means "pressed in this frame", and it is kept in the history with the move.
- Gamepad: the browser only exposes it after a button is pressed. The left stick has a radial dead zone (`STICK_DEADZONE`).
- **Touch (the user, 2026-11-12; built, `src/input.js`):** **move** is a floating analog stick on the half of the screen away from the dash: where the finger first touches is the center, the distance of the finger from it sets the speed like a gamepad stick (a dead zone in the middle, the full speed from `TOUCH_STICK_RADIUS`, in screen pixels, not scaled by the canvas), and the angle from the center to the finger is the direction; a picture of the ring and the dot is drawn while the finger holds it. **Dash** is a touch anywhere on the other half (`TOUCH_DASH_SIDE`, "right"): a round DASH button is drawn there but it is only a picture, and the whole half is the button; **the dash goes in the direction of the movement**, a swipe has no direction of its own (the user decided; the input is still `{ move, dash }` and the simulation did not change). One finger moves and a second finger dashes. A touch that starts on the bar at the top (pause, back, forward, BACK) belongs to the page. Priority of the move: gamepad stick, then touch, then keyboard. The numbers (radius, dead zone) are guesses to tune by playing on a phone. Planned by the user, not built: options for right- and left-handed players and other accessibility settings (the dash side is a named setting for that). There is no Node test (it needs the page).
- A faint grid on the ground (the agent's addition): with the camera locked on the avatar, an empty background would make movement invisible. It is unrelated to the collision grid.
- The loop in `src/main.js` runs at most one step per browser callback. On faster monitors the extra callbacks are ignored, so the game does not speed up. On slow machines it runs in slow motion.
- The HUD is plain text: the frames per second in the top-right corner (counted over one second in `src/main.js`, drawn by `drawFps`), and, in the bottom-left corner, from the bottom up: the dash (charges, the recharge count, READY; by `drawDashStatus`), the health and, only when the mecha has a shield, the shield (one decimal; by `drawHealth`), the weight the mecha carries now in KG, and one line per weapon with its name, the rounds in the magazine and in the reserve, and the reload count while it reloads (by `drawWeapons`). The user asked for numbers only, no visual work for now.
