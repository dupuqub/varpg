# Weapons and equipment, and shots and objects

## In short

- A weapon is a part with a recipe in data, so a new weapon needs no new code; it aims on its own at the closest enemy the Radar of the Head sees (docs/rules/weapons.md).
- The repeater fires at a cadence and reloads when the magazine is empty; every round weighs, so the avatar gets lighter and faster as it fires, and its push and body slam follow its weight of the moment (docs/rules/weapons.md).
- A bullet is a moving hitbox that lives until it leaves the window of the grid around the avatar or its pierce, the number of things it crosses, is spent (docs/rules/weapons.md).
- The sword winds up toward the closest enemy within reach, turning its aim partway toward it each frame, then strikes a fixed cone of slash damage, even if the target is gone (docs/rules/weapons.md).
- Every hitbox is born after everyone has moved; a bite or a blow lives one frame, and its fading trace is visual state, which the rules never read (docs/rules/weapons.md).
- A target is the closest actor with a given tag that still has health; an enemy with no target stands still (docs/rules/weapons.md).
- Bodies and attacks ignore a list of tags, and a hit lands unless the target has one: no enemy hurts an enemy, the avatar hurts no ally, attacks spare their maker (docs/rules/weapons.md).
- Shots and blows hurt and break Rocks and Shrooms, once per hitbox; the bite ignores objects (docs/rules/weapons.md).
- A bite or a blow hits all it touches, each once; a bullet hits only the first thing its path meets in a frame (docs/rules/weapons.md).

## Weapons and equipment (the user, 2026-11-11; the full reference is `docs/weapons.md`)

Read `docs/weapons.md` before touching anything about weapons: it keeps the user's vision (which earlier summaries had lost), the old project's model, kept only as a **relic, never as rules** (`docs/reference/`, read its `README.md`: a screenshot, `relic-old-project-hud.jpg`) and the open questions. The decisions, short:

- **Four weapon slots (`ShoulderL`, `ShoulderR`, `ArmL`, `ArmR`), auto-aim, one part per slot.** There are armor-only parts that cannot take a weapon; a weapon comes as the arm or shoulder itself and replaces the armor-only part. Shoulders and arms need not be weapons (wings, shields): the same parts and specs.
- **Modularity is the business goal** (blueprints and mechas are what is sold): a weapon is a recipe in data made of a few pieces (trigger, what is born, how it behaves, its own trigger, effect and emissions), so a new weapon needs no new code. A grenade or a mine is an object, a bullet is a hitbox that moves, a cluster rocket emits on its end.
- **Ammunition has weight, so the avatar gets lighter as it fires** (the weight of the avatar becomes dynamic).
- The first weapon is a simple repeater of hitboxes: limited ammunition, a reload time, a cadence.
- **The first weapon exists (the user, 2026-11-11): the repeater, the part `repeater-arm`, in the combat blueprint of Chamber 2.** A part may have a `weapon` (a recipe in `src/data.js`: auto-aim rule, frames between shots, reload frames, the rounds with their weight, magazine and reserve, and the projectile). The avatar carries `weapons` (the state of each: rounds in the magazine and in the reserve, frames to the next shot, frames of reload) and one attack per weapon (`{ base, now, projectile }`, so mods and the skill tree have the same place to enter). `fireWeapons` runs each step after the minds: it counts down, reloads when the magazine is empty, and fires at the target `pickTarget` gives for the `target` of its attack (the closest `enemy` within the Radar of the Head, the first in the list on a tie: test 134), spending a round.
- **A bullet lives until it leaves the grid (the user, 2026-11-12):** it has no life in frames (`framesLeft` is null, no `lifeFrames` in the data); it is removed when its center leaves the window of the grid around the avatar (the same area the actors wrap in), or when its pierce is spent. A bite still lives its frames.
- **A bullet is a hitbox that moves (the user's decision):** `velocity`, `struck` (each thing is hurt once) and `pierceLeft`. **Pierce is how many times it crosses (the user's rule): 0 crosses nothing and ends at the first thing it hits, 1 crosses one thing and ends at the second**; the name says the effect, not the number of targets. It is a variable of the projectile, inherited from the weapon (later the skill tree). A hitbox that does not end when it hits (a bite) has `pierceLeft` null. A dead actor is not hit and does not spend a pierce.
- **Ammunition weighs, so the mecha gets lighter and faster as it fires (the user's rule).** The avatar's weight is therefore **dynamic**; reloading changes no weight. Consequences to know: the push by weight follows the current weight, and **the body slam follows the current weight too (the user's decision)**: `now` of every attack is recalculated every frame from the weight the actor has then (`finalAttackNow`); the `base` stays as it was made. Weight comes from one place: the parts of the blueprint plus the rounds in the avatar's own weapon state (`carriedWeight`).
- The numbers (cadence, reload, rounds, damage) are guesses that err **upward on purpose** (the user: "better to look overpowered than a rubber duck"); the capacity of the Torso was raised to 8 t to leave room for ammunition and parts.
- Two kinds of weapon are wanted: a bullet that travels (crowd control, hits what is in the way, maybe stronger) and one that hits a chosen target at once (more precise, a little weaker). Only the first exists.
- Not built yet: grenades, mines, emissions at the end of a life, the damage range per shot.
- Swords (an arc), spears (a thrust with pierce) and hammers (area damage) were discussed; the preparation, an experiment and not decisions, is in `docs/weapon-families.md` (including a checklist of what to build first and open questions for the user).

## Shots and objects (the user, 2026-11-13)

A hitbox hurts a Rock or a Shroom the way the body slam does (test 189): `resolveHitboxes` takes as candidates the actors and then the objects near the hitbox (`objectsReaching`) that it touches, does not ignore (`hitboxHurts`: the tags its attack ignores; the objects carry `object`) and has not struck; a hit resolves the damage against the object (`resolveDamage`), takes it off with `damageObject` (it lives in `state.damagedObjects`), writes one journal line with the name of the object (`rock small:0,1`) and counts a pierce like any other hit. Each object is hurt once per hitbox (`struck` holds names). A broken object is gone and is not hit again (`canTakeHit`, in `landHit`). The weapons' list is `[self, ally]` and the sword's too, so shots and blows hurt objects; the bite's list has `object`, so the Biter does not. Decided (the user, 2026-11-14): Rock and Shroom act the same, and **the sword hurts and breaks Rocks and Shrooms the same way a shot does** (issue 44, test 217).

**How a hitbox hits (the user, 2026-11-14, issues 43 and 44; built, tests 213 to 219).** A hitbox says by a tag how it works: the key `hits` of its attack (the name is the coordinator's proposal, the user may rename it), one of `HITS`, refused on load when missing or unknown (`checkHits`, through `checkAttackKeys`), copied into the hitbox at its birth, read by `hitsThisFrame`:
- **`"all"`** (the sword, and the bite): everything in its contact is hit, each once, in list order (a cone over two Biters and a Rock writes three lines).
- **`"first"`** (the weapon's bullets): only the first thing its path meets in that frame is hit, actors and objects alike. "First" is measured by how far along its movement of the frame each candidate is first touched (`firstTouchAt`: the hitbox moved in a straight line by its velocity; the time of first touch of the moving circle against the candidate's circle, 0 when it already touched at the start of the frame). Ties at the same moment keep the list order (the actors in the order of `state.actors`, then the objects in the order of `objectsReaching`); a hitbox that did not move this frame, or a cone, meets everything at 0, so the list order decides. Things it cannot hit never count as first and are passed: ignored tags, its owner (`self`), the already struck, and the dead or broken (`canTakeHit`, in `landHit`: the next in the order is tried). With pierce 0 the bullet ends on that first thing; with pierce above 0 it goes on and can hit the next thing in a later frame, never the same thing twice: one hit per frame. A hit of 0 damage is still a hit (issue 46): it marks `struck`, spends a pierce and stops a bullet.
