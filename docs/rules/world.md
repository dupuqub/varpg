# The world, collision and the grid

#### The world: seed and objects

**Objects (rocks, Shrooms) are made by the seed and the formula, and are not stored in the state** (the user's decisions). They are not actors in the code (the actors are the avatar, `createAvatar`, the Dummies and the Biters, born from the spawn events of their map by `spawnEvents`). **One function makes everything that exists in a run (the user's idea, 2026-11-10):** `finalizeObject(type, rolls, blueprint, id)` takes the recipe (the entry of `src/data.js`), what the seed rolled and the blueprint, and returns the complete object; the avatar and every object from the seed come out of it.

- The seed is `state.seed` (a whole number). It is in the state so a snap alone is enough to replay.
- `objectsInArea(seed, left, top, right, bottom)` in `src/sim.js` is a pure function: it returns `{ type, layer, position, square, radius, health: { now, max }, weight, bounce }` for every object whose center is in the area, in a fixed order (layer by layer, in the order of the definitions, row by row inside each). `objectsReaching` returns every object that may **touch** the area (its center can be outside, as far as the largest radius of its layer); the collision, the drawing and the placement of Dummies use it. **The ground is made of layers (the user's idea, 2026-11-10):** a thing that lives in the seed may one day be huge and rare, and it cannot share the squares of the small ones. Each layer in `terrain.objects.layers` is complete and has its own numbers: the world is cut into squares of the size of the layer, each with at most one object (`chancePercent`), kept away from the edges of its square by `objectMargin(layer)`, so objects of one layer never overlap; no object within the layer's `safeRadius` of the starting point. The place of the layer in the list goes into the salts (`100 x place + salt`), so layers do not roll the same numbers. When objects of two layers overlap, **the layer with the larger square wins** and the other object is not placed (`isUnderBiggerLayer`; deterministic, and only the bigger layers are asked). Only one layer exists in the definitions; tests 103 to 105 build a layer of huge objects in a patched copy to prove the mechanism. The type is picked from `rareKinds` and `kinds` of the layer (`kindInSquare`), and the radius and the health are rolled inside the `size` and the `healthRange` of that type (`rollInRange`; every roll has its own salt, listed in `objectInSquare`). Looking at a bigger area never changes an object. `layer` and `square` say which object it is.
- `hashInts(seed, x, y, salt)` makes the numbers: only `Math.imul`, xor and shifts, so it is exact in every browser (no trigonometry, no `Math.random`). A **known answer** for one seed is fixed by a test, and DATA in Chamber 0 prints the same objects.
- The kinds are entries of `DEFINITIONS.objects` (the **rock**, the **Shroom** and the **Branch**), each an independent set of ranges. No object is placed by hand and no value of an object is fixed (the user's rule: no leftovers, no placeholders).
- **Objects have health (the user's decision):** each object rolls a whole number inside the `healthRange` of its type from the seed. An object comes from the formula at full health and carries it as `health: { now, max }` (the user's mold: everything with a variable amount has a now and a max: health, shield, ammunition...), both equal at creation; only a **hurt** object is in the state, in `state.damagedObjects`, a plain object from `"layer:squareX,squareY"` to its current health (the `now` of a hurt object; the object itself is made again from the seed and so keeps its full values). An object at zero is **broken** (the agent's choice; the user did not say what happens at zero): it is not drawn and nothing collides with it. The hit that breaks it has no collision response (the actor is not pushed or bounced and keeps its speed), but still hurts the actor. Health numbers are drawn above any hurt object.
- **Push between actors by weight (the user's decision, 2026-11-11, `pushShare` in `src/sim.js`):** when two actors overlap (the avatar included), each is pushed back by the other's weight over the sum of the two, so the heavier moves less: the heavy mecha pushes light enemies and heavy enemies push it (the avatar's weight is what it carries now, `carriedWeight`: the parts of its blueprint and the rounds it still holds). Two actors of weight 0 move half each. An object never moves: the actor takes the whole push. The avatar's cells are written again when it is pushed, like a Dummy's. A consequence: a Dummy can push the avatar into an object at the end of a step; the next step's collision with objects pushes it out (a standing avatar is pushed with no hit). Planned (the user's ideas, **not implemented**): the user wants the **dash force to push enemies a little and open space** when surrounded, and he wants the tension of being surrounded (the push by weight is the base of it); later three kinds of dash: normal, **blink** (goes through rocks) and **charge** (breaks rocks; `crushes` is the mechanism). Agent's cautions: pushes in a chain need a fixed order (they have one: list order) and the result of a step depends on it; the rock formula must be reviewed if rocks are ever generated outside the collision window.

#### Collision with still things

`collideWithObjects` in `src/sim.js`, called in `step` right after the movement of each actor that has `move`: **one routine for every actor, the avatar and the Dummies alike (the user's rule, 2026-11-11: a Shroom bounces everyone, it only does not hurt everyone).** The things it tests are the objects near the actor (found by the formula, not the broken ones). It says whether the actor met an object it was not touching before, and the reaction to that is the actor's own (the Dummy picks a new angle).

- Circle against circle. The *normal* is the unit vector from the thing to the actor. The actor is pushed along the normal until it just touches the thing.
- The velocity is split into the part going **into** the thing (along the normal) and the part going **along** it. The part going in becomes `-bounce x` itself, and the part along is **not changed**. So no bounce cancels the reflection only and the actor **slides** (the user's definition: "bounce 0 is not friction 1, it does not stop movement, it only cancels the reflection"), and the angle matters. Friction does not exist; it could become another value per thing later.
- **Bounce (the user's decisions):** only the **Shroom** has a bounce for now; everything else has none (the rock). A bounce is **never above 1**, or the thing would eject whoever hits it, like a spring (`assertValidDefinitions` fails above 1). The Shroom's bounce grows **in a straight line with its radius**, from a minimum at the smallest radius to 1 at the largest (`finalBounce` in `src/sim.js`, made into the object at its creation; the two ends are `bounce` in the definition).
- **Collision threshold (the user's decision):** the collision only happens when the speed going into the thing (the impact) is at least `collision.minImpact` ("1 for now", to be found by playing). Below it **nothing triggers**: no hit and no bounce, and the actor just stops against the thing (the part going in is removed). Its purpose is to avoid several contacts in a row. Bounce and weight are properties of the still actor and have nothing to do with each other: any still actor (a block in its nature, not a "rock" with sizes) has a weight (and, if it has one, a bounce).
- When the collision happens, the **body slam** hurts both sides before the bounce (see "Damage" (docs/rules/damage.md)). **An object of weight 0 is the exception (the user, 2026-11-15, issue 47: "the Branch has to break with anything"):** the body of any actor (the avatar, a Dummy, a Biter) breaks it at the first contact, with no minimum impact, no push and no bounce, and the actor keeps its speed (`breakWeightless`, asked right after `crushObject`; tests 226 and 233); a body that does not hurt objects writes a hit of total 0 on it in the journal. Any hit that lands on it breaks it too, a hit of 0 included, and a bullet spends its pierce or stops on it (tests 232 and 234; see "Weight 0" in docs/rules/damage.md).
- The objects are tested in the fixed order of `objectsInArea` (`objectsNear` keeps that order); a corner between two objects is resolved over the next frames. See "Relations" for the speed above which an object could be crossed in one frame.

#### Collision grid

- The **window** of cells around the avatar is the **whole universe of the game** (the user's decision): nothing ever exists outside these cells, and collision is tested only between actors inside it.
- The avatar is in the center cell. The window must cover **all the ground where an actor can be**, including actors that are off screen (the user's requirement: at least twice the screen). It is a rectangle of cells, odd on each side.
- Cell size, window size and the size of the screen are **dev settings**: the values are in `constants.grid` of `src/data.js` and the state keeps a copy, `state.grid`, so a run is reproducible. Do not hard-code them elsewhere. The screen size is the canvas of `index.html` written once more in the rules, because the rules need to know what is off the screen (the birth of an actor); `src/main.js` refuses to start a chamber if its canvas has another size (`startChamber`), and test 101 checks the two agree for the canvas of Chamber 1. Agent's note: a bigger window costs almost nothing, because what costs is the number of actors tested, not the area; so err on the larger side.
- Every actor **stores the cells it covers** in `actor.cells`: `{ from: { x, y }, to: { x, y } }`, a rectangle of cells with both corners included (the user's decision, 2026-11-10: any actor can have several cells, there is no separate list for big ones). It is the rectangle that the square around its circle reaches, so a small actor has one cell (up to four over a border) and a huge one has many. `step` writes it for every actor **after the movement** and before the collisions (`setCells`), and the collision reads it from the actor (the user's rule: read from the object). The cells are counted in the world, not around the avatar (see below). Whenever a collision pushes an actor (the avatar included), its cells are written again at once, so at the end of the step the stored cells are always right (a test checks it).
- Cells are counted **in the world** (cell zero is around the origin of the world; `cellIndex`), so the cells of an actor change only when it moves. A cell is always a pair of integers. The window is a rectangle in pixels around the avatar (`windowWidth x cellSize` by `windowHeight x cellSize`), which `wrapIntoWindow` enforces; the cells only serve the collision.
- Consequences (the agent's reading, to be confirmed). **Not implemented:** nothing enforces the invariant (point 1) today; point 2 is built (`wrapIntoWindow`, see below).
  1. Every actor must always be inside the window, an invariant to check (fail loudly), not a filter.
  2. An actor that would leave the window reappears on the opposite side of it. The window is centered on the avatar, so the rule is on the offset from the avatar, and it must match `cellIndex`.
  3. Nobody crosses the seam: actors on opposite edges are not neighbors. It is invisible, because the seam is off screen.
  4. The ground is infinite. Only the population is bounded.
  5. To see the limits of the universe, a debug zoom-out is the way; drawing every cell only produces noise. (The user no longer wants to see the collision boxes.)
- **Who uses the grid:** the collision between actors: between the actors that move (the Dummies and the Biters), and between one of them and the avatar (`collideActors`; the objects are met earlier in the step, in the movement). Two actors are tested only if they share a cell. The index (cell to the actors that move, not the avatar, that cover it) is made again every step from `actor.cells`; it is not state. The result is **exactly** the one of testing every pair in list order (tests compare the whole state), including an actor pushed into a third one in the same step: that is why the cells are written again after every push and the next actor to test is asked again after every test (`nextInCells`). Keep that property: the grid is a faster way to the same result, never a different rule. The collision with **objects** still finds them by the formula of the seed (`objectsNear`), not by the grid; with many Dummies that search is now the largest cost of a step.
- The wrap exists for every actor except the avatar (`wrapIntoWindow`): the window is the real area of the game, and an actor that leaves it on one side comes back on the opposite side. The invariant for every actor is still not checked.
